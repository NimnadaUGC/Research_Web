Problem Definition & Motivation

Understanding the gaps in Business Analysis support and the constraints of resource-limited SMEs

The Business Analysis Challenge

Business Analysts in SDLC

BAs sit at the critical intersection of stakeholder needs and development, translating ambiguous business requirements into clear, executable specifications. In Agile and traditional environments alike, they play an essential intermediary role.

But they face pressure: ambiguous language, tight budgets, competing stakeholder priorities, and limited AI tools tailored to BA workflows.

The AI Opportunity

Generative AI offers powerful capabilities for automating documentation, suggesting clarifications, and surfacing risks. However, most AI tools are developer-centric, lacking BA-specific workflows, ethical safeguards, and transparency mechanisms.

The opportunity: Design AI-enhanced methodologies that augment (not replace) BAs while maintaining human oversight and ethical accountability.

Three Core Problems

Ambiguous Requirements

Requirements elicitation often results in vague or incomplete specifications. Generic AI tools fail to ask targeted clarification questions specific to BA context—missing critical details about business rules, edge cases, and constraints.

Ethical Risk & Low Transparency

AI-generated artifacts can embed bias in language, prioritization, and assumptions. Teams lack trust without explicit transparency—which stakeholders were considered, what compromises were made, and where assumptions lurk.

Alignment & Handover Friction

Communication gaps between stakeholders, BAs, and developers lead to rework, delays, and misaligned deliverables. SME teams with tight budgets and limited AI skills face compounded friction—they need low-cost, reproducible solutions.

Literature Gaps

Across 40+ peer-reviewed sources and recent SLRs (2023–2026), we identified three critical gaps:

Developer-Centric AI

Most generative AI literature targets coding assistants. Scant research on BA-focused adaptive prompting or iterative elicitation workflows that reduce ambiguity in artifacts like user stories and SRS documents.

Ethics at Artifact Level

AI ethics literature focuses on model training and fairness. Few lightweight, artifact-level ethics rubrics exist for checking bias in BA outputs—user stories, acceptance criteria, and requirements specifications are rarely audited for hidden assumptions or fairness concerns.

SME Context Missing

Limited end-to-end hybrid workflows designed for resource-constrained Sri Lankan SMEs. Existing AI-BA solutions assume corporate budgets for enterprise tools—not the low-cost, reproducible toolkits needed in emerging economies.

Gap Summary

There is no published first hybrid BA agent framework combining adaptive prompting + ethics + human-in-the-loop workflows validated in SME settings. This research addresses that gap.

Research Questions & Objectives

Main Research Question (MRQ)

How can AI-enhanced methodologies and hybrid agents improve Business Analysis work while ensuring ethical safeguards and preserving the BA's crucial human role?

SQ1: Elicitation

How well do adaptive prompt workflows reduce ambiguity and improve clarity and completeness in requirements artifacts?

Target: 20–40% improvement in clarity rubric scores (measured against manual baseline).

SQ2: Ethics

Can ethical prompting plus bias checks improve transparency and stakeholder fairness in BA output?

Target: 15–30% reduction in bias flags + improved stakeholder satisfaction (via survey).

SQ3: Hybrid Agents

Do human-in-the-loop hybrid agents boost BA efficiency and developer alignment in Agile SME contexts?

Target: 30–50% efficiency gain + improved BA–developer alignment (measured by task completion time & defect counts).

Specific Context: Sri Lankan SMEs

Constraints

  • Limited AI skills / training budget
  • Low capital for expensive enterprise tools
  • High velocity but frequent requirements churn
  • Resource-constrained teams (often 3–10 developers + 1 BA)
  • Pressure to deliver quickly with minimal risk

Opportunity

  • Open-source, reproducible toolkit (low cost, high adoption)
  • Local relevance → potential to pilot and iterate rapidly
  • Real-world validation → credible for upscaling to regional SMEs
  • Ethical and transparent approach → builds trust in resource-limited settings
  • Practical guidance → empowers BAs without deep AI background
